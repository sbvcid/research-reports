## 1. Executive summary

### The question

In September 2026 the market pushed MUFG's (8306) PBR from roughly 1.6x to 1.88x. The research question was narrowed to one sentence:

> **Under the currently verified earnings and capital-constraint assumptions, what exactly is the market paying for — and what has to be true for this price to hold?**

### The core valuation frame

The causal chain established after the eleventh pass is not a conventional three-stage valuation. It is:

```
Business / interest rates
    ↓
EPS, ROE (already established as true)
    ↓
The market broadly accepts this earning power
    ↓
The genuine free variable = PER / PBR
    ↓
Future shareholder return
```

**Key finding (FACT / DERIVED):** between 2026-05-15 and 2026-09-07, MUFG's forward ROE was essentially flat (12.51% → 12.57%) while its PBR rose 14.37%. **The market did not re-price ROE improvement — it re-priced the multiple.**

### Final base case

With exit PER held at 14.9x, three-year annualised total return:

| Scenario | Annualised total return |
|---|---:|
| Company FY2025 payout policy maintained (60.7% total payout ratio) | **8.86%** |
| Company FY2026 actual authorisation (43.8% total payout ratio) | **9.70%** |

**Base case band: 8.86% – 9.70% annualised total return** (DERIVED; ASSUMPTION: ROE 12.11% and PER 14.9x held constant).

### The single most important outcome driver

**Exit PER.** On an identical earnings path:

| Exit PER | Annualised total return |
|---|---:|
| 12.0x | 1.86% |
| 14.0x | 6.78% |
| 14.9x | 8.86% |
| 16.0x | 11.30% |

**The multiple moves about 7 percentage points. Every payout-related variable combined is worth 0.84 percentage points.**

### The largest downside driver

**Multiple compression to 12x.** Even on the most investor-friendly low-payout scenario (g_EPS 7.07%), a 12x exit PER yields only about 2% annualised total return.

**Additional FACT:** MUFG's current PBR of 1.873x is higher than every one of the seventeen year-end observations on record (previous high 1.32x at 2026-03-31), and PBR÷ROE is above the entire five-year year-end range by 22%.

### Conclusions the evidence supports

1. **The earnings improvement is real.** Over five years NII contributed 51.4% of profit growth and fees 42.5%, together 94% (DERIVED from FACT).
2. **But the market did not re-price it.** Forward ROE was flat across the re-rating window (DERIVED).
3. **The capital constraint is real.** CET1 was 9.2% at 2026-03-31, below the company's own 9.5% floor (FACT, verbatim from company IR).
4. **That constraint directly drove the FY2026 buyback cut from the ~¥500bn scale down to ¥100bn** (FACT, company states it is "considering CET1 ratio").
5. **The market is not pricing MUFG's dependence on Morgan Stanley** (DERIVED from FACT).

### Still unknown

1. The market cost of equity for this name — **never observed**, and seven passes confirmed that no defensible PBR can be cited
2. The path of the exit PER — **this report does not forecast it**
3. Whether the 9.5% CET1 floor is a hard constraint or a soft one
4. What proportion of future RWA growth "earns well"
5. The 25% gap between MUFG's disclosed quarterly equity-method income and its 23.95% stake

## 2. Investment thesis

**This section is not a recommendation, not a rating, and contains no price target.** It describes *what the market must currently believe* and *where that belief is supported and where it is fragile*.

### What the market must currently believe

At a share price of ¥3,700, the market must simultaneously believe three things:

1. **ROE can be sustained around 12%** — this is the company's own medium-term target (FACT, IR verbatim: 「東証定義 ROE12%程度 かつ PER の維持・向上」, *"ROE around 12% on a TSE-defined basis, and maintenance/improvement of PER"*).
2. **The market will keep paying 14.9x forward PER** — higher than any year-end PBR in MUFG's seventeen-year record.
3. **The earnings contributed by MS will not reverse** — 32%–39% of net income over five years (DERIVED from FACT).

**Of the three, only the first is even partly within the company's control.**

### How MUFG's earnings, capital allocation and per-share growth connect

The causal chain this research established (FACT / DERIVED):

```
ROE 12.11% (rolled forward from a consensus base)
    ↓
Payout: dividends 40.1% + buybacks 3.7% ~ 20.6% = total payout ratio 43.8% ~ 60.7%
    ↓
Retained portion compounds at ROE → equity growth
Buyback portion → share count reduction → EPS accretion
    ↓
g_EPS
    ↓
At a fixed PER, the share price grows at the same rate as g_EPS
    ↓
Plus dividend cash
    ↓
Annualised total return
```

**No step on this chain is a "re-rating."** Every step is the company earning the money back.

### What a reasonable return looks like at a 14.9x exit PER

**ASSUMPTION:** ROE 12.11%, PER constant at 14.9x, dividend payout held at 40.1%.

Three-year total return, annualised: **8.86% – 9.70%**, depending on whether the total payout ratio lands at 43.8% or 60.7%.

**Composition of that number:**
- roughly 6.2 – 7.1 percentage points from per-share EPS growth
- roughly 2.7 – 2.9 percentage points from dividend cash

### The effect of multiple compression on the outcome

| Scenario | Annualised total return |
|---|---:|
| exit 12.0x | 1.86% |
| exit 12.0x (low-payout scenario) | about 2.0% |
| exit 14.9x | 8.86% |
| exit 16.0x | 11.30% |

**Moving from 14.9x to 12.0x removes about 7.0 percentage points of annualised return.**

### Why the payout assumption matters less than the exit multiple

Two numbers side by side settle it:

| Variable | Range it can move through | Impact on annualised return |
|---|---|---:|
| Total payout ratio (43.8% – 60.7%) | 16.9 percentage points | **about 0.84pp** |
| Exit PER (12.0x – 16.0x) | 4.0 turns of multiple | **about 7pp** |

**The payout ratio can move across 17 percentage points and deliver less than 1 percentage point of return. The multiple can move 4 turns and deliver 7 percentage points.**

The reason is structural: at ROE 12.11%, retained earnings compound at 12.11%, while buybacks accrete EPS at 1/PER = 6.71%. **Shifting payout from dividends to buybacks barely changes the per-share outcome; moving the multiple from 14.9 to 12 changes the terminal price enormously.**

### Three growth rates that must never be conflated

**This is the most important structural element of the model in this report.**

| Symbol | Name | Formula | Value (high payout / low payout) |
|---|---|---|---:|
| **g_B** | Equity and net income growth | ROE × (1 − total payout ratio) | 4.76% / 6.81% |
| **g_S** | Share count change | −buyback payout ratio ÷ PER | −1.38% / −0.25% |
| **g_EPS** | **EPS growth** | (1 + g_B) ÷ (1 + g_S) − 1 | **6.23% / 7.07%** |

**The identities behind these:**
- g_S = −b/p, because buyback spending of b×NI retires (b×NI)÷P shares, and P = p × EPS = p × NI/S
- g_NI equals g_B, because at a constant ROE net income grows at the same rate as equity
- In steady state BVPS grows at the same rate as EPS, because B and NI grow together and the share count is the common denominator

### Why the three cannot be collapsed into one

**This research made all three of these errors at some point:**

| Error | Where | What was wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | `8306-valuation` | g = ROE × (1 − 40.1% dividend payout) = 7.25%. **Used the dividend payout ratio only** |
| Definition | `8306-multiple-anchor` | g = ROE × (1 − 60.7% total payout) = 4.75%. **Caught the direction of the problem, but this is net income growth, not per-share growth** |
| **Correct** | `8306-return-model` | Split into three quantities; **for a per-share investor the answer is g_EPS = 6.23%** |

**All three attempts (7.25% → 4.75% → 6.23%) were wrong, in different directions.**

**A second-layer error: the effect of buybacks on book value.**

```
BPS_new = (B − Pq) / (S − q)
Δ(BPS)/BPS = (q/S)(1 − PBR) / (1 − q/S)
```

FACT — MUFG's PBR is 1.873. At q/S = 1.38%, the calculation gives **Δ(BPS)/BPS = −1.22%**.

> **Editor's note (English edition).** The sealed Chinese original prints **−0.94%** at this point. The source pass's own calculation sheet (`8306-return-model/.../data/return_model.md`) prints **−1.22%**, and substituting the stated inputs into the stated identity gives (0.0138 × (1 − 1.873)) ÷ (1 − 0.0138) = **−1.22%**. The −0.94% in the sealed report appears to be a transcription error; it is reproduced here corrected to −1.22% with the discrepancy disclosed rather than silently fixed. **The conclusion the number supports is unchanged and directionally identical:** when PBR > 1, buybacks reduce book value per share.

**When PBR is greater than 1, buybacks reduce book value per share.** MUFG trades at 1.87x, so buybacks are not book-value accretive. Plugging the total payout ratio directly into a retention formula **is not a valid identity.**

## 6. Valuation

### This report refuses to cite a "fair PBR"

FACT — the four methodological conclusions of the `8306-valuation` pass:

1. The common g = 2% assumption implies a payout ratio of 82.9%, which contradicts the official 40.1% → **withdrawn**
2. Substituting a self-consistent g makes the Gordon model pathological
3. The well-behaved substitute is to invert the problem: the expected return implied by the current price
4. **The cost of equity has never been observed**, and the Gordon form is pathological for this name → **no single fair PBR should be quoted**

**This position survived twelve passes without being overturned. This report maintains it.**

| Exit PER | Annualised total return |
|---:|---:|
| 12.0x | **1.86%** |
| 13.0x | 4.38% |
| 14.0x | **6.78%** |

| Scenario | Total payout ratio | Buyback share of payout | g_EPS | Annualised total return |

## 7. Return decomposition

### The four components

| Component | Nature | Magnitude (high / low payout) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **EPS growth** | per-share | 6.23% / 7.07% | g_EPS (model derivation) |
| **Dividend cash** | **cash** | 2.86% | 40.1% dividend payout ÷ PER |
| **Share count reduction** | per-share | already inside g_EPS | **must not be added again** |
| **Multiple change** | **valuation** | scenario axis | **never added into a return** |

### Three boundaries that must not be crossed

**① Dividends are a cash return.** The shareholder receives cash directly.

**② Buybacks affect EPS through share count, not through cash.** The shareholder receives no money; the benefit appears as a larger claim on earnings.

**③ If buybacks are already inside EPS growth, they cannot also be counted as a separate return component.**

**This error actually occurred.**

### The effect register

The checkable form of Rule 16 of my research protocol (added to this project after the tenth pass):

| output_id | effect_id | representation | occurrences |
|---|---|---|---:|
| g_B | `E_RETAINED_EARNINGS` | per-share | 1 |
| g_S | `E_SHARE_COUNT` | per-share | 1 |
| g_EPS | `E_EPS_GROWTH` | per-share | 1 |
| Dividends | `E_DIVIDEND_CASH` | **cash** | 1 |
| Buybacks | `E_SHARE_COUNT` | per-share | **same effect as g_S** |
| Multiple | `E_MULTIPLE` | **valuation** | scenario axis, not an addend |

**Hard rule: the same `effect_id` may appear only once in a given decomposition.**

**This check immediately caught an error whose arithmetic was entirely clean:** one pass added "dividends 2.69% + buybacks 1.38% = shareholder yield 4.08%" into total return, even though `E_SHARE_COUNT` was already inside g_EPS. **That figure has been formally invalidated.**

**The register forces a distinction that narrative tends to blur:**

> `E_MS_CAPITAL_DRAG` (capital side) and `E_EPS_GROWTH` (earnings side) **are both driven by Morgan Stanley's profits, but in opposite directions**. A strong MS pushes EPS up and CET1 down.
>
> **Collapsing them into "MS is good" or "MS is bad" is a definitional error.**

### The correct decomposition

```
Annualised total return = g_EPS (per-share) + dividend yield (cash)
         = 6.23% + 2.86% → compounded 8.86% (high payout)
         = 7.07% + 2.86% → compounded 9.70% (low payout)
```

`E_SHARE_COUNT` appears **only inside** g_EPS.

## 8. What the model actually says

**If every currently verified assumption holds, what genuinely determines the outcome?**

### Risk decomposition

| Variable | Impact | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Earnings / retention assumptions | Limited | Fully modelled (g_B enters the formula) |
| Payout / buyback mix | Limited to moderate (0.84pp) | Fully modelled |
| CET1 / capital constraint | Moderate (sets the payout level) | Causality closed through to the FY2026 round |
| **Exit PER** | **Dominant (about 7pp)** | **Not modelled as a forecast — this is an honest blank** |

### The core conclusion

> **Improvement in operating assumptions does not eliminate valuation multiple risk.**
>
> Multiple compression from 14.9x to 12x affects annualised return **far more than any change in the current payout assumptions.**

**This conclusion has been stable across twelve passes, and each pass has strengthened it:**

- First stated in the eighth pass ("the multiple is a level risk, not a growth requirement")
- Ninth pass added the historical anchor (the current PBR is above all seventeen year-end observations)
- Tenth pass found it **more** valid once the true g_EPS of 6.23% replaced the assumed 10% per year
- Eleventh pass quantified it (payout 0.84pp versus multiple 7pp)
- Twelfth pass confirmed that closing the causality on the capital constraint does not change the ranking

### This is neither "cheap" nor "expensive"

**FACT** — MUFG's PBR÷ROE is 7.8% cheaper than its own sector, and it is cheaper on PER.
**FACT** — MUFG's PBR is above every year-end observation in seventeen years, and its PBR÷ROE is above the entire five-year year-end range.

**Both are facts.** That is precisely the substance of the valuation multiple question: **there is no single "cheap/expensive" answer, because the answer depends on what multiple you are willing to hold a bank at.**

## 10. Unresolved questions and research limits

**This report does not answer any of the following.**

### Valuation

1. **The market's cost of equity for MUFG** — never obtained in twelve passes. **This is the direct reason the Gordon model cannot be cited.**
2. **The path and probability of the exit multiple** — direction only, no probabilities. The historical multiple series from IRBANK was collected as annual snapshots (five observations), not as a full distribution.
3. **The 8% – 10% cost-of-equity range is an analyst-set plausible range, not an observation.**

### Capital

4. **Whether the 9.5% CET1 target is a hard constraint or a management goal** — management's wording ("best to return to the lower range as soon as possible") reads soft, but cutting the buyback reads hard. **This report does not adjudicate.**
5. **How much of future RWA growth is in high-return assets** — MUFG discloses the rotation (cutting low-yielding business, adding high-yielding business), but **planned and achieved figures cannot be matched from the sources obtained**. This determines whether ROE improvement and shareholder returns move in the same direction.
6. **Whether the capital cost of MS earnings growth has long-run significance** — the specified-item deduction rises with MS retained earnings, so **the stronger MS gets, the tighter CET1 becomes**. How this scales over time is not modelled.
7. **Whether Shriram Finance is one-off or part of a broader India investment plan** — if the latter, the capital constraint is structural. The investment plan was not obtained.
8. **Whether the FY2026 H2 buyback is executed** — this directly determines whether the base case lands at the 8.86% end or the 9.70% end.
9. **RWA growth rate versus internal capital generation rate** — determines the long-run sustainable payout level.

### Business

10. **The 25% gap between MUFG's quarterly equity-method income and its 23.95% stake** — see Section 5.
11. **The cause of the PBR move from ~1.6 to 1.88 between 2026-03 and 2026-09** — once TOPIX was added in the seventh pass, the abnormal return against all four beta benchmarks was **positive** (+0.15% to +0.22%), which **refutes** the "already priced in" inference. But **the cause of the jump itself remains unexplained.**
12. **The regional bank cross-section** — only partial data (79 rows, mean PBR 0.58 / ROE 5.3% / PER 9.9, all third-party). The JPX sector index is 68% the three megabanks, so "sector" here mostly means the three megabanks themselves.
13. **The 7x contradiction between Q1 JGB-related gains/losses and the full-year figure** — Q1 FY2027 was −¥35,036mn, annualising to roughly −¥140,000mn, whereas full-year FY2024 was −¥991,400mn. Basis or time distribution has not been separated.
14. **MUFG's adjusted ROE** — Mizuho discloses an "adjusted ROE (実力値) of 10.5%"; MUFG and SMFG make no equivalent disclosure. ROE rankings therefore carry a caveat.

### Methodological limits

15. **Every sensitivity table in this report assumes ROE, PER and the payout ratio are constant.** The real path will not be.
16. **September was a rotation month; interest-rate transmission and sector rotation cannot be separated within a single window.**
17. **The historical multiple series is an annual snapshot, not a distribution.** "Above the five-year range" holds on five observations.
18. **Most of this report's key derivations come from MUFG's own account (earnings call).** This is the company's explanation of its own numbers, not third-party audit.

## 11. Falsification and update triggers

**This report should be reopened if any of the following occurs.**

### Capital

| Trigger | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **CET1 still below 9.5% at FY2026 year end** | Management committed to returning to the range in H1. A miss invalidates the "the constraint is temporary" reading, and the payout may be constrained further |
| **H2 buyback not executed and the FY2027 authorisation cut again** | The base case should be revised down to the low-payout end or below |
| **The 9.5% floor is explicitly lowered, or management publicly describes it as a soft target** | Directly changes the weight of the capital constraint |
| **A large investment beyond Shriram Finance** | The capital constraint becomes structural rather than one-off |

### Valuation

| Trigger | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Any market cost-of-equity observation obtained** | The largest unresolved gap in twelve passes. Once obtained, the pathology of the Gordon model can be re-evaluated |
| **PER breaks below 13x or moves above 16x** | Enters directly at the two ends of this report's sensitivity table |
| **MUFG changes its policy language on PER** (for example abandoning "maintain/improve") | The company's own medium-term target is one of this report's supporting arguments |

### Business

| Trigger | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **MS ROTCE falls materially from 26.6%** | The flat scenario holds, and the inference that flat MS equals −10.4% is validated |
| **The 25% quarterly equity-method gap is resolved** | Affects every absolute-value estimate of MS contribution |
| **ROE deviates from the 12% target** | Forward ROE of 12.11% is the input to every model here |
| **The structure of RWA growth changes** (the high-yielding additions target is met or missed) | Determines whether ROE improvement and shareholder returns move in the same direction |
| **Dividend / buyback policy clearly changes** | The payout end of the base case moves |

### Methodological

| Trigger | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **The effect register rules prove insufficient** — for example a third representation of the same economic effect appears | The double-counting control would need redesign |
| **The cause of the PBR move from 1.6 to 1.88 is explained** | Affects the strength of the conclusion that the multiple is the dominant variable |
